## Design Context

### Users
Mix of recruiters/hiring managers, fellow developers, and potential freelance clients. Portfolio needs to scan well quickly (recruiters) while showing personality and technical depth (devs) and trustworthiness (clients). Viewing context is typically desktop during work hours (recruiters) or mobile any-time (devs).

### Brand Personality
Reserved at first glance, playful once you dig in — mirrors Zoe's actual personality. 3 words: **grounded, editorial, quietly-playful**.

### Aesthetic Direction
- **Primary palette**: Deep forest greens (#0E1A12 through #6AAA7A) with a deep maroon accent (#58141C) and amber pop (#C89628)
- **Theme**: Default light mode, dark mode toggle available
- **Anti-references**: AI-gradient corporate portfolios, floating particles, terminal themes, "hello world" hero text, soulless minimalism, pill badge grids, identical card layouts
- **Positive references**: GoRocky.ph (warm, earthy, card-based), Maya.ph (vibrant accent against neutrals), GoTyme (clean hierarchy), SZA's ocean album cover (depth, mood)
- **Direction**: Magazine editorial with bite. Not calm literary journal — art-directed magazine. Typography-driven, asymmetric, content-first.

### Design Principles
1. **Authenticity over flash** — Every visual choice should feel deliberate, not decorative
2. **Content-first** — Projects and achievements are the hero, not the UI
3. **Editorial rhythm** — Vary layouts between sections like turning magazine pages. No monotonous grids
4. **Earned distinction** — Maroon appears only for achievements (best thesis, hackathon win). Green handles everyday UI. Amber adds life
5. **Reserved then playful** — Composed first impression, personality reveals as you scroll deeper
6. **Typography does the work** — Use type hierarchy, weight, and scale instead of containers and badges
